    Abbreviations

    • @ → at  (e.g. @Yosemite → at Yosemite)
    • w/ → with
    • w/o → without

    Phrases

    • in <city> → City  (used by Locate for lat/lon)
    • at / near <place> → Where  (stadium, park, trail, route — text only)
    • for <activity> → What For
    • with <people> → Who With
    • Commas stay inside the value (e.g. with Seth, Christian); only the next keyword or a semicolon ends a phrase.

    Dates

    • Month D, YYYY  ·  Month YYYY  ·  D Month YYYY
    • YYYY Month [D]  ·  YYYY-MM-DD  ·  M/D/YYYY  ·  M/D/YY
    • Ordinal days like 1st, 2nd, 3rd are understood.
    • A weekday prefix or suffix is ignored if present.

    Visit Levels & Tags

    • Any active level name in the text auto-selects that level.
    • Any active visit label name auto-selects that label.

    Multi-part Shortcut

    • Split with ||, |, ::, ;, or - :
      Where | What | Who

    Anything left over is placed in Additional Details.

    Import and Export

    Data is saved in this local browser only.
    Use the Import/Export features to move your data between Devices and Browsers.
    - JSON (.json): full data backup; re-importable to other Devices/Browsers.
    - Markdown (.md): structured text; use for pretty rendering or HTML based applications.
    - Rich Text (.rtf): formatted text; for word processors with basic formatting.
    - Plain Text (.txt): simple text; used anywhere, useful for basic notes.

    JSON restores your full map. Text exports are for sharing, notes, or printing.

    Toolbar

    1. Use the app header buttons to show or hide Map, Legend, and Notes.
    2. Use Rename to change the map title. Blank names fall back to Your Trail Log.
    3. Use Tip for the optional drink prompt, Add as App for device-matched install directions, and Settings for appearance, dates, note tags, imports, and exports.
    4. Returning users may see a What's New banner and red dots for feature releases. Open What's New or dismiss the banner to clear them.
    5. What's New includes Release Notes and the Roadmap. The Roadmap accents ticket pills for ideas that already have a written plan, and Release Notes keep detailed updates under clean headed sections.
    6. Hold Shift + Control + Option to temporarily show shortcut badges. Developer Tools lists hidden commands such as H H for restoring dismissed hints and the update banner while testing.

    Map

    1. Tap a location to mark it. In cycle mode, the first tap on an already marked location shows it in Notes; the next tap cycles levels.
    2. Tap the map title to switch between US and World. The button shows the destination and loading progress while the next map is prepared.
    3. Use the Map Labels control to switch states, territories, countries, or a linked Waypoint Pack between no labels, abbreviations, and names. World Name labels use short English atlas names, fit smaller countries, and add leader lines for tiny dot-backed places.
    4. Use Scroll Map to pan and zoom. Type an exact zoom percentage, use the zoom buttons, or reset to 100%. Your zoom, pan center, and fit/scroll mode are saved.
    5. Use Fit Map when you want the map framed cleanly for screenshots or sharing.
    6. Use View Locations to show or hide note pins. Notes without coordinates sit at the center of their state or territory shape; exact coordinates place markers more precisely.
    7. Use Match Notes to dim map locations and pins that do not match the active Notes filters.
    8. Use Rangefinder on the US or World map to pick saved note pins as Start and End points, draw Drive or Plane planning rings, and compare the straight-line distance between two places.
    9. Rangefinder rings can be filled or stroke-only, clipped to the active land area, shown in miles or kilometers, customized up to eight distances, and shown with or without Drive or Plane time estimates. The ring-style menu stays open while you combine fill and clip options; click outside it when finished. The time menu lets you adjust the average speed for the current travel mode.
    10. Mapped markers open the note when there is one note. Nearby markers cluster, zoom when they can split, or open a picker when they are too close.
    11. Alaska, Hawaii, and territory points use inset map scale. Mainland rings clip away from those insets, inset starts clip to their own inset, and cross-inset Start-to-End comparisons use real coordinates with a broken map cue instead of a route line.
    12. In Wayfinder, use Packs to preview curated place lists on the map, assign priorities, attach matching notes, and add selected places as normal Wayfinder notes.
    13. On desktop, drag the divider between the map and cards to resize the working area.

    Legend

    1. The top Legend level is the highest priority and wins the map color when a location has multiple levels.
    2. Use the Legend to edit level names, definitions, colors, order, and whether a level is excluded from stats.
    3. If no level is assigned to Wayfinder, turning Wayfinder on opens a picker for your existing levels and recommends Want to Visit. The chosen level is automatically excluded from completion totals.
    4. Drag levels anytime to reorder them. Turn Edit on to reveal row controls, or swipe a row right-to-left for quick Edit/Delete.
    5. Deleting a level moves affected locations down to the next lowest level.
    6. Click a Legend level to filter Notes by that level. Click it again to clear the filter.
    7. The icon beside the overall Legend totals shows what those stats measure: US locations, World countries, or the linked Waypoint Pack currently selected in Map Labels.
    8. When Map Labels targets a linked Waypoint Pack, the Legend keeps your same levels and colors but shows pack-note counts and percentages instead of state or territory totals.
    9. Use Auto Colors to apply a palette to the current level order. Suggested categories can be shown or hidden, or you can make your own levels.
    10. On desktop, use the Legend Position button or drag the Legend title to place it around the map.
    11. Default levels are Visited and Not Interested. Not Interested is excluded from completion stats.

    Notes

    1. Use Notes search to find locations by name, type, level, date, coordinate status, tag, or note details.
    2. Use Quick Add beside search to add a note from the main list. Pick the target location in the note header, then Save or Save and Add Another.
    3. Right Arrow cycles the Alphabetical and Chronological sort. The Priority button adds exact, higher, or lower filtering, 1-to-5 or 5-to-1 ordering, and priority grouping on top of that base sort. When the filter is All, the priority number stays cleared because it does not affect the list. Down Arrow cycles views; the view shortcut also works inside an individual location.
    4. Use Detailed, Expanded, Condensed, or Text Only view. Detailed shows every populated note field and the full details text; click that text to edit it inline, then press Cmd/Ctrl+Enter or use the checkmark to save. Text Only is copy-ready for Apple Notes.
    5. Open a location to manage its levels and Visit Notes. The same four view buttons appear in the Visit Notes header, which stays pinned while its notes scroll. In Detailed or Expanded, select the note count to collapse or reveal that location's notes.
    6. Use the icon chips to filter by Location Tags. Empty circle means notes without an icon; the ellipsis opens Location Tag settings.
    7. In Wayfinder, the Packs button opens an unselected pack browser over Notes. Pick an Available Pack to show its saved icon/overlay/label settings and labeled map preview, then search locations, set priorities, attach notes, or Add / Refresh Selected.
    8. Right-click or long-press an icon chip to show only that icon. Do it again when one icon is selected to restore all icons.
    9. Use the date filter to cycle between All Dates, Year Only, Year and Month Only, and Full Dates Only.
    10. Use the coordinate filter to cycle between All, Mapped, and Missing.
    11. Use the circle-slash excluded toggle to show or hide locations that only have excluded-from-stats levels. Amber means excluded locations are visible.
    12. Use Smart Convert in the note editor to turn quick text into weekday-aware dates, levels, city, where, people, purpose, and details.
    13. Saved City, Where, What, and Who values appear as local suggestions while you type.
    14. Use Locate beside the Lat/Lng row to look up latitude/longitude (City + state). The Where field for a stadium or trail name stays text-only. The arrow button next to Where moves its text into City. You can also edit or clear coordinates manually.
    15. Notes support City, Where, What, Who, Additional Details, partial dates, priority, and multiple icon labels. Four or fewer labels keeps rows easier to scan.
    16. When one location is open, Notes shows every note for that location. Main-list search, level, icon, date, coordinate, and visibility filters do not hide that selected location's notes.

    Basecamp

    1. Open Basecamp from the toolbar or press B. Basecamp is a local workspace for trip planning, packing lists, gear notes, and other writing that is not tied to a single location.
    2. Use + New Pad to create up to twenty named pads. Click the header title to rename a pad, click the header icon to choose a pad icon, and use the per-pad Delete button to remove a pad.
    3. Drag a sidebar pad row, or focus a row and press Alt+Up / Alt+Down, to reorder pads. The first pad opens automatically when Basecamp is launched.
    4. The Format, Font, and Text toolbars cover headings, body, monostyled blocks, dashed/bullet/numbered/ checklist lists, emphasis, highlight, shadow, double underline/strikethrough, sizing, baseline, alignment, justification, quote levels, and independent indent. Hold Cmd or Ctrl to reveal shortcut chips on toolbar buttons.
    5. Use the Other toolbar group for Link, Code Block, and Clear Formatting. Image is reserved for a future update. Cmd/Ctrl+Z and Cmd/Ctrl+Y undo and redo edits and toolbar formatting inside the editor.
    6. Search across all pads in the sidebar. Press / to focus search. Matches highlight temporarily and stay selectable. Switching pads or clearing the search returns the editor to its normal view.
    7. Use Link Note in the editor header to attach existing US or World location notes to the active pad. Linked notes show inline with quick open and unlink controls; editing a linked note keeps Basecamp open behind it.
    8. Use Copy Pad to copy the active pad, or Copy All Pads to copy every pad in order. Markdown, Rich Text, and Plain Text exports include a per-pad Basecamp section. JSON backups always include every pad.
    9. Upgrading from 4.3.0 turns your single Basecamp scratchpad into the first pad titled "Basecamp Pad" with the default Basecamp pad icon. The migration runs once and never overwrites pads you have already created.

    Other

    1. Add dates as a year, month and year, or full date. Settings controls date order and month-name vs numeric formatting.
    2. Use Settings > Appearance > Text Size to tune readability without changing map zoom.
    3. Use JSON Import/Export in Settings to back up data or move it between browsers or devices.
    4. Export formats: JSON is a full backup; Markdown is structured text; Rich Text opens in Word or Pages; Plain Text pastes anywhere.
    5. Use Add as App to choose a light or dark icon and get install directions for iPhone/iPad, Android, Mac, or PC. Mac icon reload guidance appears at the top of the Mac instructions when needed.
    6. The app restores your visible panels, selected Notes location, Notes sort/view/grouping/filter choices, collapsed categories, map zoom, map pan, and map mode.

    FAQ: Data

    Where is my data saved?
    In your browser localStorage on this device only. Nothing leaves your machine.

    Can someone else use the same app link?
    Yes. Each browser keeps its own separate local copy of the data.

    Does it work offline?
    Yes. Once the file is open, no network connection is needed. The optional Locate button needs internet access for lookup, but manual coordinates and saved markers work offline.

    Can I move data to another device?
    Export a JSON backup in Settings, then import it in the app on the other device.

    Can I undo a reset?
    No. Export a JSON backup first if you want a safety net.

    FAQ: Map

    Will my map zoom come back?
    Yes. The app saves your scrollable-map zoom level, fit/scroll mode, and last pan position.

    What is Match Notes?
    It applies the active Notes filters, including date precision, to the map, dimming locations and hiding note pins that do not match.

    Why are some pins grouped?
    Nearby mapped notes cluster so the map stays readable. Click a cluster to zoom or open the picker when notes are too close to separate.

    Why do some World labels have leader lines?
    Tiny countries and island territories may use a small offset label with a line back to their map dot so the whole-world view stays readable.

    Are Rangefinder rings routing distance?
    No. Rings and Start-to-End comparisons use straight-line distance. Drive and Plane times use your selected average speed, starting from 60 mph for Drive and 600 mph for Plane, so both are planning hints.

    Do Waypoint Packs change state progress?
    No. Pack pins, labels, priorities, and Legend pack stats come from linked notes. States and territories only change when you edit their levels yourself.

    FAQ: Legend

    What's the difference between cycle and select mode?
    Cycle taps rotate through active levels. Select mode opens a picker so you can choose a level and add a note.

    Can I have more than five levels?
    No. Five is the current cap. There is a Roadmap item for adding more levels later.

    Why do some levels show a no symbol instead of a percentage?
    Those levels are excluded from completion stats.

    What does the icon beside the overall Legend stats mean?
    It identifies the current stats group: US locations, World countries, or the linked Waypoint Pack selected in Map Labels.

    FAQ: Notes

    Will my Notes filters come back?
    Yes. Notes view, sort, grouping, excluded visibility, date precision, coordinate filter, Priority filter/order/grouping, Legend filters, icon filters, collapsed categories, and selected location restore with your local settings.

    What does Categorical do in Notes?
    It groups locations by City, State, and Territory while keeping your selected Alphabetical or Chronological sort inside each group. Group by Priority temporarily takes precedence when it is active, and a location can appear in multiple priority groups when its notes have different priorities.

    What does Detailed view show?
    Detailed is the full-card view. It shows the location's levels and note/date totals, then location precision, level-colored tags, compact Location and Event summaries, and the complete details text without truncation. Linked Waypoints also show website, photo, and priority actions.

    Where does Priority show up?
    Priority appears in the note editor, Detailed cards, Condensed priority controls, Expanded/list icon badges, map pins, Text Only view, and Markdown/Rich Text/Plain Text exports.

    Can I edit a Detailed card without opening the note editor?
    Yes. Click its details text, make the change inline, then press Cmd/Ctrl+Enter or use the checkmark to save. The live counter tracks the 2,000-character limit. In the full note editor, Enter adds a new line and Cmd/Ctrl+Enter saves.

    What is Text Only view for?
    It renders a clean plain-text version of the current Notes view that is easy to select, copy, and paste into Apple Notes.

    How does Smart Convert work?
    Type a quick note, then Auto Convert fills recognized date, level, visit icon, where, what, and who fields. Weekdays next to dates are understood. Leftover text goes to Additional Details.

    Can one note have multiple visit icons?
    Yes. Pick any labels that fit, though four or fewer keeps Notes rows easier to scan.

    Where do extra Location Tags come from?
    More Icons is built from the circle symbols bundled into this file. Search by icon name, label, or related terms, then add the ones you want.

    What happens when I attach an existing note to a Waypoint?
    The note stays a normal note. Trail Log links it to the pack, updates managed map details like coordinates, appends the pack note, and preserves your level, date, people, topics, and other details.

    What is the camera spot under a Waypoint photo?
    When you open a Waypoint Pack photo, Trail Log can check the photo's Wikimedia Commons metadata for where the camera stood and, when available, which direction it faced. This is an optional online, read-only lookup; it never changes the note's own latitude/longitude, and photos without camera metadata show a quiet unavailable state.

    How do icon categories work?
    The chip strip under the search box scopes More Icons to one or more categories. Tap a chip to add or remove that category, tap All to clear, and right-click or long-press a chip to isolate just that category. Search is scoped to the chips you have selected.

    Can two Location Tags use the same hotkey?
    No. If a key is already assigned to another active tag, the app warns you and keeps the existing shortcut setup unchanged.

    FAQ: Other

    What is Basecamp?
    Basecamp (toolbar button, or press B) is a local workspace for planning that is not tied to one place. Create up to twenty named pads with their own icons, format text with the Format, Font, and Text toolbars (headings, lists, checklists, links, quotes, alignment, sizing), reorder pads with drag or Alt+Up/Down, search names and content across pads, and link existing US or World location notes for quick reference. Pads autosave and are included in Markdown, Rich Text, and Plain Text exports plus JSON backups.

    What happened to my old Basecamp scratchpad?
    The first time 4.4.0 loads, its text becomes the first pad titled "Basecamp Pad" with the default Basecamp pad icon. The migration runs once, keeps the original paragraphs, line breaks, and last-updated timestamp, and never overwrites pads you have already created.

    Are Basecamp pads encrypted or synced to a cloud?
    No. They stay in this browser with the rest of Trail Log's local data. Use JSON backups to move or protect them.

    How do I add it like an app?
    Use Add as App in the top toolbar. Trail Log detects iPhone/iPad, Android, Mac, or PC, expands the matching directions, and keeps one other device guide open at a time.

    How do hints work?
    Hints can be dismissed one at a time, or turned off globally in Settings. Turning Hints back on restores dismissed hints.
